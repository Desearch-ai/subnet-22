# Desearch 2.0 — participation and progress

**Updated:** 2026-09-06

## What changes now?

This direction update does not announce a change to weights, reward lanes, registration, hardware requirements or customer endpoints. That includes existing AI-search, X and social routes. It is not a program-opening notice. No operator action or equipment purchase is requested by this proposal.

Subnet reward changes and customer API changes are separate decisions. Any actual change requires its own notice.

For the work being proposed, read [direction and first phase](02-direction-and-first-phase.md).

## What this means for you

- **Miners and model builders:** the opportunity is intended to expand from executing defined work to improving methods. No new work is opened by this update. Before committing resources, you should be able to inspect the actual requirements, evaluation and reward terms described below.
- **Validators:** credible assessment remains important. New task types need usable evidence, clear access rules and affordable checking; a weight submission is not approval to deploy a model or index to customers. The [assessment and release explanation](company/open-network-and-bittensor.md#evaluation-and-decision-responsibilities) describes the distinction.
- **SN22 supporters and stakers:** assess whether useful contributions are reaching the product and whether continuing a program is justified. Participation counts or a larger index alone are not proof. The [network rationale](company/open-network-and-bittensor.md#why-bittensor) explains the strategic relationship, and [value flows and rights](company/open-network-and-bittensor.md#value-flows-and-rights) explains what it does—and does not—mean economically.

## Where your feedback helps now

Which sources or search problems deserve the first bounded tasks? What would make contribution assessment credible and practical? Which requirements or rights would you need to understand before participating?

These questions invite input on program design; they do not request submissions, investment or work under unspecified terms.

## Before a program opens

Miners, model builders and validators should receive a usable program notice before being asked to incur costs. It must explain:

- what work is open, eligibility, software and measured hardware/operating requirements;
- the accepted output, evaluation method, relevant comparison and who makes each decision;
- what earns credit or reward, when it qualifies, and how rejection, replacement or retirement affects it;
- authorship, contributor rights, permitted reuse and what will be released;
- material team participation, conflicts and any differing eligibility or reward treatment;
- how to challenge a decision and how changes to terms are communicated.

Execution, comparative evaluation and production deployment can have different acceptance criteria. Terms must make that distinction understandable to each participant.

A transition notice must additionally identify affected operators or customer routes, what carries over, the effective event, support and rollback arrangements. Program terms should describe expected costs and limits without implying guaranteed profit.

## How we will report progress

We will provide a written community update every week. Updates will distinguish design decisions, tests and live releases, with dated evidence, limitations and the next checkpoint. This communication cadence is separate from benchmark execution and is not a promise of a new release every week.

The [first-phase checkpoint](02-direction-and-first-phase.md#the-first-reviewable-checkpoint) describes the proposed evidence to prepare. This proposal does not set a program-opening date.

Readers should be able to inspect actual outputs and adopted contributions, understand whether search improved, and see the costs or failures that changed our decisions. More activity or a larger collection alone would not establish success. Relevant evidence will accompany the milestone it supports.

A limited pilot may have its own terms before wider deployment. Operator requirements and reward terms will be communicated before new work is requested; customer migration, where needed, receives its own notice.
