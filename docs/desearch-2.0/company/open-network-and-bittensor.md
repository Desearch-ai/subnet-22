# Desearch: open network and Bittensor

A useful web index needs continuing work: finding sources, cleaning and updating information, and improving how relevant material is retrieved. We want people with different expertise to help build that foundation, not only respond to individual queries.

The [company foundation](company-foundation.md) defines the ambition. This chapter explains why an open network is part of it.

## Why an open network

There are two complementary opportunities:

- **Build and maintain useful data.** Source specialists and operators can add coverage, improve extraction and keep important information current. Accepted information can support repeated searches and other permitted uses.
- **Improve the technology.** Researchers and model builders can propose better representations and ranking methods. Once adopted, an improvement can benefit many requests without its author serving each one.

Data work can create lasting value even before a new model wins a competition. Better methods can make an existing collection more useful. These contributions need appropriate, distinct evaluation.

The intended relationship is:

**Useful customer problems → contribution opportunities → credible assessment → maintained data and adopted methods → better products and new uses.**

The purpose is to widen the pool of people who can improve the system. The team still has to choose useful problems, integrate work and serve customers well.

## Why Bittensor

SN22 gives Desearch an existing setting for independently operated miners, validators and protocol incentives. We want to apply that setting to recurring work that improves a retained asset: useful web information and the technology for finding it.

Different contributors can specialize. A source operator may improve coverage or refresh reliability; a model builder may later improve relevance; a validator may develop more credible and efficient checking. The opportunity is continuing participation across those problems, with evaluation, credit and applicable rewards.

Bittensor is therefore part of how we intend to build the product, not an unrelated label on an API. Its value must appear in useful contributions, better coverage, adopted improvements and viable participant economics.

The protocol does not decide which pages customers need or automatically discover the best model. Desearch must design the tasks and evaluation. The [incentives overview](../desearch-2.0/04-incentives-and-rewards.md) explains how assessed work would connect to subnet rewards.

Each program needs useful results and viable costs, including the work of checking and integrating contributions. We will use that evidence to improve tasks, expand useful work or stop work that does not justify continuing.

The [first-phase plan](../desearch-2.0/02-direction-and-first-phase.md) explains how initial data collection and preparation can lead to a maintained search product and later opportunities to improve models and ranking. Success means useful information and methods reaching the system, not simply more completed tasks or a higher isolated model score.

## Evaluation and decision responsibilities

The proposed design separates subnet assessment from product adoption. [Incentives and rewards](../desearch-2.0/04-incentives-and-rewards.md#from-completed-work-to-rewards) explains the work-to-reward path; [the technical overview](../desearch-2.0/03-first-phase-technical-overview.md#how-accepted-work-becomes-searchable) explains the separate release path into customer search. Neither is a claim that 2.0 is deployed.

Desearch defines the problems and participation terms. Contributors identify their outputs, configuration, origins and usage rights. Evaluators produce evidence under stated methods; they may be validators or other designated assessors.

The team remains accountable for release, security and rollback. Assessment can inform rewards without authorizing customer deployment. Consensus alone does not establish source truth, usage rights or evaluator independence.

Valid execution can qualify under announced terms without inventing a better method. Exploration may be supported before its outcome is known. Funding a pilot, accepting work, continuing a program and deploying a result are separate decisions; a later product decision must not rewrite terms already promised for completed work.

## What open should mean

| Area                 | Intended principle                                                                                                                              |
| -------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Participation        | Discoverable opportunities with stated eligibility, requirements, costs and terms                                                               |
| Evaluation           | Published objectives, methods and material results; protected tests and private customer material remain protected                              |
| Credit and decisions | Attribution, reasons and a challenge route; disclose material team participation and conflicts                                                  |
| Models and code      | Develop appropriately licensed open-source search models and aim to make adopted work openly reusable                                           |
| Data and serving     | Explain sources, versions and actual checks; distinguish permitted search use from redistribution and training uses                             |
| Product control      | Disclose central operation; apply the same relevant product criteria to internal and external candidates, with blind comparison where practical |

These are program principles, not claims that all assets or procedures have been released. Code, weights and source material can require different permissions. An operated index is not automatically a freely downloadable dataset.

## Value flows and rights

The intended ecosystem benefit is useful work that lasts: maintained information, reusable model/code releases where permitted, contributor credit, learning from experiments and better customer products. Selected data services could also support other AI systems or subnets.

Customers pay Desearch for API use or contracted services. Protocol rewards go to eligible participants under applicable rules. Contributors supply work under explicit usage terms; Desearch provides opportunities, assessment, feedback and approved releases.

Company revenue, company cash costs, participant costs and protocol incentives must be accounted for separately. Product success does not automatically establish token appreciation, a right to company revenue, equity or ownership of contributed assets. No return or participant profit is promised.
